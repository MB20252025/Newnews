MYSTERY OF THE 'CARTOGRAPHER' DEEPENS

Professor's disappearance linked to ancient maritime map discovered in Whitby

By Karla Censine · Senior Reporter
Professor Arthur Vale in his study at Whitby Archives, weeks before his disappearance.

Professor Arthur Vale, 58, a renowned historian and cartography expert, has been missing from his Whitby home for over a week. Colleagues reveal he recently uncovered a hidden map in the town's archives that he believed held the key to a centuries‑old maritime conspiracy.

"He mentioned a mysterious figure known only as 'The Cartographer'," said Dr. Miriam Shaw, a close colleague. "He became increasingly secretive in the weeks before he vanished. He told me he had found something that would change everything we know about the coastal trade routes."

Vale's study was found ransacked, with rare documents and several 17th‑century maps missing. Police are treating the case as a high‑priority investigation and conducting extensive searches across Whitby and surrounding coastal areas.

The map he found wasn't just old — it was deliberately hidden. Someone didn't want it found.

Detective Inspector Helen Crawley confirmed that forensic teams are examining the scene for any trace evidence. "We are appealing for anyone who may have seen Professor Vale in the days before his disappearance, or anyone with information about the missing documents, to come forward," she said.

Star Bakery's Night Owl Boom

By Bill Board · Advertising & Media

A small Lincoln bakery has become a late‑night hotspot after extending its hours to serve shift workers and night owls. Owner Lila Grant said the idea came after noticing empty streets but growing online demand. Staff joke the bakery is "on star mode all night," with croissants disappearing faster than they can be counted. City officials are monitoring the trend, though staff insist nothing unusual is happening despite ovens "working overtime on their own."

"They Refuse To Listen"

By Paige Turner · Senior Features

A local resident admitted to giving motivational speeches to her houseplants. Neighbours report they "remain stubbornly unmotivated," despite promises of water and fertiliser bribes. The resident, who wished to remain anonymous, said she began the practice after reading a study that claimed plants respond to positive reinforcement. "I've been doing it for months, but they still look exactly the same," she lamented. "I think they're just ignoring me."

Students Build Solar Car from Scrap

By Al Beback

A group of engineering students has built a fully functioning solar‑powered car using mostly recycled materials. The car successfully completed a 15‑mile test drive. Team leader Aisha Khan said the project aimed to highlight sustainable innovation on a budget. Plans are underway to refine the design for a national competition.

Chef Arrested Over Triangular Pizza

By Chris P. Bacon

A local chef was arrested after his triangular pizza experiment caused chaos in a city restaurant. Head chef Marco Bellini claimed it was a "harmless attempt to challenge traditional pizza geometry." Diners reported slices that "rejected circular reality entirely." The restaurant has been closed for inspection.
